## Introduction

In previous chapters, we examined what recommender systems are, how they operate, and why they have become an essential component of modern digital platforms. However, every artificial intelligence model relies on a source of information from which it learns. In recommender systems, this source consists of the data generated by users and their interactions with the system.

To accurately predict user preferences and provide relevant recommendations, recommender systems collect and analyze various forms of user data. These data are generally classified into two major categories: **explicit feedback** and **implicit feedback**. Understanding the characteristics, advantages, and limitations of these two types of data is fundamental to designing effective recommendation algorithms.

---

## Explicit and Implicit Feedback

Recommender systems seek to infer user preferences by observing either what users directly communicate or how they behave while interacting with a platform.

**Explicit feedback** refers to information that users intentionally provide to express their opinions, preferences, or levels of satisfaction. Examples include assigning ratings, liking or disliking content, and writing reviews or comments.

**Implicit feedback**, in contrast, is derived from user behavior rather than direct statements. Browsing history, click patterns, watch time, purchase records, search queries, and other behavioral signals serve as indirect indicators of user preferences.

In essence, explicit feedback represents what users consciously tell the system, whereas implicit feedback reflects what the system can infer from users' actions.

---

## Explicit Feedback

### Definition

Explicit feedback occurs when users deliberately communicate their preferences to a system. Because the information is intentionally provided, it generally offers a clear and direct representation of user interests.

Common forms of explicit feedback include:

- Numerical ratings (e.g., assigning one to five stars)
- Likes and dislikes
- Product or content reviews
- Comments and written evaluations
- Favoriting or bookmarking content

For example, users of IMDb can rate movies using a star-based scoring system. Similarly, music streaming services may allow users to indicate their preferences through "like" or "favorite" buttons.

### Advantages of Explicit Feedback

The primary advantage of explicit feedback is its **high interpretability**. Since users directly express their opinions, the resulting data provide a relatively accurate representation of their preferences. This reduces ambiguity and allows recommendation algorithms to learn more precise user profiles.

Additional advantages include:

- Clear indication of user satisfaction
- Easier interpretation by machine learning models
- Higher confidence in inferred preferences
- Reduced uncertainty compared to behavioral signals

### Limitations of Explicit Feedback

Despite its accuracy, explicit feedback suffers from an important limitation: it requires active user participation.

Many users are unwilling to continuously rate products, review content, or provide detailed feedback. Consequently, explicit feedback datasets are often sparse, containing relatively few observations compared to the total amount of content available.

A well-known example is Netflix. The company initially employed a detailed star-rating system but later replaced it with simpler "thumbs up" and "thumbs down" options. This change reduced the effort required from users while still allowing the platform to collect valuable explicit feedback.

---

## Implicit Feedback

### Definition

Implicit feedback consists of information collected from user behavior without requiring users to intentionally express their preferences.

Rather than asking users what they like, the system observes what they do.

Examples of implicit feedback include:

- Clicking on products or content
- Viewing webpages
- Watching videos
- Listening to songs
- Adding items to a shopping cart
- Purchasing products
- Conducting searches
- Scrolling through pages
- Replaying content
- Skipping content
- Time spent viewing specific items

For instance, if a user watches a movie from beginning to end or repeatedly listens to a particular song, the system may interpret this behavior as evidence of interest, even though no explicit rating was provided.

### Advantages of Implicit Feedback

One of the greatest strengths of implicit feedback is its abundance. User interactions are continuously generated as part of normal platform usage and can be collected automatically at a large scale.

Benefits include:

- Large volumes of available data
- Continuous real-time collection
- Coverage of nearly all users
- Rich behavioral information
- Ability to capture evolving preferences

Modern online platforms routinely record millions of behavioral events each day, making implicit feedback an invaluable source of information for recommendation algorithms.

### Limitations of Implicit Feedback

Although abundant, implicit feedback is inherently noisy and ambiguous.

A particular action does not always correspond to genuine interest or satisfaction. For example:

- A user may watch an entire movie but dislike it.
- A customer may add an item to a shopping cart and later abandon the purchase.
- A user may click on a recommendation out of curiosity rather than genuine interest.
- A song may be played repeatedly because it appears in an automatically generated playlist rather than because the user enjoys it.

As a result, interpreting implicit feedback requires careful modeling and often involves uncertainty.

---

## Comparing Explicit and Implicit Feedback

Explicit and implicit feedback differ significantly in terms of quality and quantity.

### Accuracy versus Ambiguity

Explicit feedback provides a direct expression of user preferences and is therefore generally more accurate and less ambiguous.

Implicit feedback, on the other hand, relies on behavioral interpretation. Since user actions can have multiple explanations, the resulting data may contain considerable uncertainty.

### Data Scarcity versus Data Abundance

Explicit feedback is often limited because users rarely provide ratings or reviews consistently.

Implicit feedback is generated naturally through user interactions and therefore exists in much larger quantities.

### Strengths and Weaknesses

|Explicit Feedback|Implicit Feedback|
|---|---|
|High accuracy|High volume of data|
|Easy to interpret|Automatically collected|
|Direct preference indication|Rich behavioral information|
|Requires user effort|Contains noise and ambiguity|
|Often sparse|More difficult to interpret|

In practice, modern recommender systems typically combine both types of feedback to leverage their complementary strengths.

## Real-World Applications

### Netflix

Netflix utilizes both explicit and implicit feedback to personalize content recommendations.

Explicit feedback is collected through simple positive and negative preference indicators, such as "thumbs up" and "thumbs down." At the same time, Netflix gathers extensive implicit feedback, including:

- Viewing history
- Watch duration
- Time of viewing
- Device type
- Content selected after recommendation
- Recommended titles that were ignored

Netflix places considerable emphasis on implicit feedback because it provides a detailed and continuous picture of user behavior.

### Amazon

Amazon's recommendation engine combines explicit and implicit signals to suggest products.

Explicit feedback includes customer ratings and product reviews. Implicit feedback is gathered from actions such as:

- Product searches
- Page visits
- Shopping cart additions
- Purchase history
- Browsing behavior

For example, if many customers view and subsequently purchase a product, Amazon may increase the likelihood of recommending that item to other users with similar interests.

### Spotify

Spotify categorizes user signals into explicit and implicit feedback mechanisms.

Examples of explicit feedback include:

- Saving songs to a personal library
- Adding tracks to playlists
- Sharing music with others

Examples of implicit feedback include:

- Listening duration
- Replay frequency
- Song skips
- Listening patterns over time

By analyzing these signals, Spotify constructs detailed user preference profiles and recommends songs, albums, artists, and playlists tailored to individual tastes.

---

## Integrating Explicit and Implicit Feedback

Neither explicit nor implicit feedback alone is sufficient to fully capture user preferences.

Explicit feedback provides precise and reliable information but is often limited in quantity. Implicit feedback supplies abundant behavioral data but introduces uncertainty and interpretation challenges.

Consequently, most modern recommender systems integrate both forms of feedback. Explicit data improve the accuracy of user preference estimation, while implicit data provide the scale and diversity necessary for continuous learning.

The intelligent combination of these complementary data sources enables recommender systems to uncover hidden user preferences, adapt to changing interests, and deliver increasingly relevant recommendations over time.

---

## Conclusion

Data serve as the foundation of every recommender system. These data generally originate from two primary sources: explicit feedback, which consists of direct user expressions of preference, and implicit feedback, which is inferred from user behavior.

Explicit feedback offers clarity and precision, whereas implicit feedback provides scale and behavioral richness. Because each type possesses unique strengths and limitations, contemporary recommender systems rely on a combination of both to build accurate user models and generate personalized recommendations.

Understanding the nature of these data sources is essential for appreciating how recommender systems learn, adapt, and ultimately improve the user experience across platforms such as Netflix, Amazon, and Spotify.
