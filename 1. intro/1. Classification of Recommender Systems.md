Recommender systems can be categorized according to the underlying algorithms and mechanisms they use to generate recommendations. Each category possesses unique characteristics and is suitable for different application scenarios.

In this chapter, we introduce three of the most important categories of recommender systems and provide a high-level overview of their fundamental concepts. More detailed discussions of each approach will be presented in subsequent chapters.

---

# Collaborative Filtering Recommender Systems

Collaborative Filtering (CF) is one of the most widely used recommendation techniques. In this approach, recommendations are generated by analyzing and aggregating the preferences, behaviors, or opinions of users with similar interests.

The core assumption behind collaborative filtering is that users who exhibited similar behavior in the past are likely to share similar preferences in the future. Consequently, items preferred by a group of similar users can be recommended to a target user.

Collaborative filtering can generally be divided into two primary approaches:

## User-Based Collaborative Filtering

Consider an online marketplace such as Digikala or Amazon. Suppose you are searching for components to build a high-performance gaming setup in preparation for the release of a highly anticipated game.

You browse items such as:

- A high-end gaming laptop
- A gaming mouse
- A gaming keyboard

By analyzing your interactions with these products—such as viewing product pages, adding items to your wish list, or placing products in your shopping cart—the recommendation system identifies other users who have demonstrated similar behavior.

If these similar users have also interacted with additional products, the system assumes that you may be interested in those products as well and recommends them accordingly.

In other words, user-based collaborative filtering focuses on finding users with similar preferences and leveraging their collective behavior to generate recommendations.

### Example

If Users A and B both purchased a gaming laptop and a gaming mouse, and User A additionally purchased a gaming headset, the system may recommend the gaming headset to User B.

---

## Item-Based Collaborative Filtering

Now consider a different scenario. Suppose you are interested in purchasing an ASUS ROG Strix G15 gaming laptop and spend considerable time reviewing its specifications or add it to your shopping cart.

By analyzing historical purchasing patterns across millions of users, the system discovers that customers who purchased this laptop frequently also purchased products such as:

- Razer DeathAdder V2 Gaming Mouse
- Logitech G Pro X Mechanical Keyboard

Rather than identifying similar users, item-based collaborative filtering focuses on discovering relationships between items themselves. The algorithm identifies products that are commonly viewed, purchased, or interacted with together.

As a result, when a user expresses interest in the ASUS ROG Strix G15, the system recommends related products that are strongly associated with it.

This approach is particularly effective in large-scale systems where finding highly similar users becomes computationally expensive.

### Example

Amazon's famous recommendation section, _"Customers who bought this item also bought,"_ is a classic example of item-based collaborative filtering.

---
## The Cold Start Problem

One of the most significant challenges of collaborative filtering is the **Cold Start Problem**.

A cold start occurs when insufficient data is available about a user or an item. For example, when a new user registers on a streaming platform such as Netflix or Filimo, the system initially has no information regarding that user's preferences.

Since collaborative filtering relies heavily on historical interactions, the absence of sufficient behavioral data prevents the system from generating highly accurate recommendations.

Similarly, newly added items may not receive recommendations because no interaction data exists for them yet.

# Content-Based Filtering

Unlike collaborative filtering, which relies on the behavior of multiple users, **Content-Based Filtering** focuses on the characteristics of items and the preferences of a single user.

In this approach, each item is represented by a set of descriptive features, such as:

- Keywords
- Categories
- Technical specifications
- Tags
- Metadata

The system builds a profile of the user's interests based on previously consumed items and recommends other items with similar characteristics.

This method is especially useful when rich descriptive information about items is available.

---

## Example: Movie Recommendation Platform

Suppose you frequently watch science-fiction and action movies involving themes such as:

- Space exploration
- Artificial intelligence
- Robots
- Futuristic societies

A content-based recommender system analyzes the attributes of the movies you have watched.

Each movie may contain information such as:

### Genres

- Science Fiction
- Action
- Fantasy
- Drama

### Actors

- Tom Cruise
- Liam Neeson
- And others

### Directors

- Christopher Nolan
- Steven Spielberg
- And others

### Keywords and Tags

- Space Travel
- Artificial Intelligence
- Robotics
- Future
- War

Based on these characteristics, the system constructs a user profile that may be summarized as:

> Interested in science-fiction action movies involving artificial intelligence, robotics, and space exploration.

The recommender system then searches its catalog for movies with similar attributes.

For example, if you have watched:

- _Interstellar_
- _Blade Runner 2049_
- _Ex Machina_

the system may recommend:

- _Arrival_
- _Dune_
- _Westworld_

because they share similar themes and content characteristics.

### Advantages

- Does not require data from other users.
- Can recommend newly added items.
- Provides personalized recommendations even for users with unique tastes.

### Limitations

- May lead to **overspecialization**, where users repeatedly receive similar recommendations.
- Often lacks diversity and may fail to introduce users to entirely new interests.

# Hybrid Recommender Systems

Hybrid Recommender Systems combine two or more recommendation techniques to leverage their strengths while minimizing their weaknesses.

The most common hybrid approach combines:

- Collaborative Filtering
- Content-Based Filtering

The outputs of these methods can be integrated in various ways, such as weighted averaging, ranking fusion, or model-based combination techniques.

A well-known example is Netflix's recommendation system, which successfully employed hybrid techniques during the Netflix Prize competition.

---

## Example: Online Bookstore

Imagine that you have recently joined an online bookstore specializing in educational content and technical literature.

Because you are a new user, the system has very little behavioral information about you, resulting in a cold start situation.

### Step 1: Content-Based Recommendations

During registration, you specify interests such as:

- Machine Learning
- Python Programming
- Artificial Intelligence

Using this information, the system recommends books containing related keywords and topics, such as:

- _Machine Learning with Python_
- _Introduction to Artificial Intelligence_

These recommendations are generated based on content similarity.

### Step 2: Collaborative Filtering Recommendations

As you continue interacting with the platform by:

- Purchasing books
- Browsing categories
- Adding books to favorites

the system gradually collects behavioral data.

Over time, it identifies users with similar interests and begins recommending books that those users enjoyed, even if those books fall outside your initially stated interests.

For example, the system may discover that users similar to you also enjoy:

- Philosophy of Science
- History of Computing

and consequently recommend these subjects as well.

### Final Recommendation Strategy

The hybrid system combines recommendations from both approaches to create a more comprehensive and accurate recommendation experience.

This combination improves recommendation quality while reducing problems such as:

- Cold Start
- Limited Diversity
- Sparse Interaction Data

# Summary of Recommender System Categories

|System Type|Mechanism|Strengths|Weaknesses|Common Applications|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|**Collaborative Filtering**|Generates recommendations based on user-item interaction patterns and similarities among users or items.|Discovers hidden patterns; does not require item features.|Cold start problem; data sparsity; scalability challenges; popularity bias.|Amazon, Netflix, e-commerce platforms.|
|**Content-Based Filtering**|Recommends items with characteristics similar to those previously preferred by the user.|Works well for new items; highly personalized; independent of other users.|Overspecialization; limited diversity; requires rich item metadata.|Netflix genre recommendations, Spotify content-based suggestions.|
|**Hybrid Recommender Systems**|Combines multiple recommendation techniques to exploit their advantages and reduce individual weaknesses.|Higher accuracy; improved diversity; better cold-start handling; greater flexibility.|Increased computational complexity and implementation cost.|Netflix, Amazon, Spotify, Filimo.|
